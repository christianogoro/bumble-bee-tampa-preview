Window Treatments for Davis Islands: Waterfront Living, Smart Choices

Davis Islands homeowners face unique window treatment challenges from UV, salt air, and waterfront glare. Here's what works and what doesn't.

I walked into a Davis Islands home last year where the afternoon sun had done more damage than any hurricane. The homeowner, a surgeon at Tampa General, had spent on Brazilian cherry hardwood floors three years earlier. The west-facing side of his living room, the side with the big picture windows overlooking the water, had faded to a completely different shade than the rest of the floor. It looked like two different types of wood.

His leather sectional had one arm that was noticeably lighter than the other. The artwork on the west wall had shifted colors. Even the paint looked different where the sun hit directly versus the shaded portions of the room.

“I thought the windows would be enough,” he told me. His home had double-pane, low-E glass. Good windows. But good windows alone don’t stop UV damage when you’re dealing with Tampa’s afternoon sun bouncing off Hillsborough Bay and blasting through your living room for five straight hours every day.

That’s Davis Islands in a nutshell. Beautiful waterfront living with some very real challenges that most people don’t think about until the damage is already done.

The Davis Islands Environment

Davis Islands is a pair of man-made islands in Hillsborough Bay, just south of downtown Tampa. The location is incredible. You’ve got water views, proximity to downtown, Peter O. Knight Airport, the Davis Islands Yacht Club, and a tight-knit community feel that’s hard to find this close to a city center.

But living on an island in Tampa Bay means your home is fighting a constant battle against the elements.

UV exposure is relentless. Davis Islands is surrounded by water, and water reflects UV radiation. A west-facing window in Davis Islands isn’t just getting direct afternoon sun. It’s getting sun reflecting off the bay surface. That’s a double dose of UV, and it accelerates fading on floors, furniture, fabrics, and artwork significantly compared to inland homes.

Salt air corrodes and degrades. You’re on an island. The air carries salt, especially during windy days. That salt air gets into everything and attacks materials that aren’t designed for coastal conditions. Real wood window treatments in a Davis Islands home will have a shorter lifespan than the same product installed in a Carrollwood home ten miles inland.

Glare off the water. The view is why you live on Davis Islands. Nobody wants to block it. But the glare coming off the bay, especially in the morning on the east side and afternoon on the west, can make rooms unusable without some kind of light management.

Hurricane and storm exposure. Being surrounded by water means more wind exposure during storms. Window treatments don’t replace impact glass or storm shutters, but they need to withstand the pressure changes, humidity spikes, and occasional water intrusion that come with tropical weather.

The Housing Mix on Davis Islands

The architecture on Davis Islands covers a wide range. The original homes from the late 1920s and 1930s are Mediterranean Revival style, similar to what you find in Hyde Park and South Tampa. Many of these have been renovated extensively, but some still retain original features including arched windows and coral rock details.

From the 1950s through the 1970s, ranch-style homes and mid-century designs were added. These tend to have larger windows and simpler lines.

Then there’s the new construction wave. Over the past 15-20 years, a lot of the smaller original homes have been torn down and replaced with modern waterfront builds. These newer homes feature expansive glass, sliding wall systems, and window walls designed to maximize the view.

Each era of home requires a different approach:

Home EraTypical Window FeaturesBest Treatment Approach
1920s-1930s MediterraneanArched tops, deep-set openings, coral rock sillsCustom shutters, Roman shades
1950s-1970s Ranch/Mid-CenturyStandard double-hung, large picture windowsComposite shutters, roller shades
2000s-Present New ConstructionFloor-to-ceiling glass, sliding walls, clerestoryMotorized solar shades, sheer shadings

Products That Survive and Perform on Davis Islands

Not every window treatment product is cut out for island living. I’ve learned this through experience, including a few early installations that didn’t hold up as well as I expected. Here’s what I recommend now and what I avoid.

Composite Shutters: The Davis Islands Workhorse

If I could only install one product on Davis Islands, it would be composite plantation shutters. Not real wood. Composite.

Norman’s Woodlure composite shutters are my go-to for this area. The material is engineered to resist moisture, and it won’t warp or crack in the humidity that Davis Islands homes deal with year-round. The finish is UV-resistant, so the color holds even on south-facing and west-facing windows.

Graber’s composite shutters are another solid option. Slightly different aesthetic, same performance characteristics for coastal environments.

Real wood shutters look beautiful on day one, but on Davis Islands, day 365 is a different story. I installed hardwood shutters in a Davis Islands home about four years ago at the homeowner’s insistence. She wanted the natural grain and warmth of real basswood. Within 18 months, the shutters on her bathroom window had started to bow, and the ones on her kitchen window near the sink were showing signs of moisture damage along the bottom rail. We replaced them with composite, and she hasn’t had an issue since.

Solar Roller Shades: View Protection Without View Destruction

Here’s the dilemma every Davis Islands homeowner faces: you bought the house for the view, but the sun coming through those view windows is destroying your home’s interior and making your rooms uncomfortable.

Solar roller shades solve this perfectly. They reduce glare and block UV radiation while preserving your view of the bay. From inside, you can still see the water, the boats, the sunset. But the intensity is managed.

The key number to pay attention to is the openness factor. Solar shade fabrics come in different openness percentages, typically ranging from 1% to 14%.

Openness FactorUV BlockedView ClarityBest For
1%~99%Visible but mutedRooms with extreme sun exposure
3%~97%Good visibility, reduced glareLiving rooms, offices
5%~95%Clear view, moderate glare reductionDining rooms, kitchens
10-14%~86-90%Nearly transparent, light glare reductionRooms where view is the priority

For most Davis Islands waterfront homes, I recommend 3% openness on west-facing windows and 5% on other orientations. That gives you strong UV and glare protection on the worst exposure while keeping a clear view elsewhere.

Insolroll makes some of the best solar shade fabrics on the market. Their ThermoVeil line has a reflective backing that bounces heat before it enters the room, which makes a noticeable difference in your heat gain.

Motorization: Not Optional for Waterfront Homes

I’ll say this plainly: if you’re in a newer Davis Islands home with large glass expanses, you need motorized window treatments. You need them because you have dozens of windows, many of them oversized or in hard-to-reach positions. You need them because the sun’s angle changes throughout the day and throughout the year, and manually adjusting 15 shades twice a day isn’t going to happen.

Hunter Douglas PowerView is the leading system, and it works flawlessly with their solar shades, cellular shades, and Silhouette shadings. You set scenes and schedules from your phone. “Morning” raises all the shades. “Afternoon” drops the west-facing shades to 50%. “Movie night” closes everything. It integrates with Alexa, Google Home, Apple HomeKit, and most smart home systems.

decision on motorization pays for itself in discipline. Manual window treatments only work if you actually use them. Most people don’t. They leave the shades up because they want the view, and then the sun damages their floors and furniture. Motorization removes the human laziness factor from the equation.

The Waterfront Floor Rescue

Let me finish the story from the opening. Dr. Patel’s Brazilian cherry floors were already damaged, but we could prevent further fading and eventually let the protected areas catch up to the faded sections as all hardwood floors naturally darken over time.

We installed Insolroll solar roller shades with a 3% openness factor on every west-facing window in the house. There were nine windows total across the living room, dining room, and a guest bedroom. All motorized with Hunter Douglas PowerView integration so they’d lower automatically at 1 PM and raise at sunset.

For the east-facing master bedroom, we went in a completely different direction. He wanted total darkness for sleeping, since he sometimes gets home from late surgeries at 2 or 3 AM and needs to sleep through the morning. We installed Hunter Douglas Duette Architella cellular shades with the LightLock system. Room-darkening doesn’t begin to describe it. With LightLock, the shade channels sit flush against the window frame and block light from the sides, top, and bottom. The room goes jet black.

The kitchen, which faces south, got Graber composite plantation shutters. The kitchen sees the most humidity in any home, between cooking steam and dishwasher cycles. Composite was the only responsible choice.

Six months later, Dr. Patel sent me a photo of his living room floor. The fading had stopped, and the previously protected areas were beginning to darken toward the faded sections. Give it another year or two and the difference will be barely noticeable. His furniture is holding its color. His artwork is stable.

Total project for the project was meaningful, but as he put it, it was a fraction of what it would refinish those Brazilian cherry floors and replace a leather sectional.

Salt Air and Material Selection

I want to spend a minute on this because it’s specific to Davis Islands and other waterfront Tampa locations.

Salt air doesn’t just affect wood. It affects metal hardware too. The tilt mechanisms, mounting brackets, and control chains on window treatments all contain metal components. In a standard inland installation, these components last the full life of the product without issue.

On Davis Islands, I’ve seen standard steel components start showing rust spots within three or four years. This is why I specify products with aluminum or stainless steel hardware for any installation on the islands. Norman and Hunter Douglas both offer corrosion-resistant hardware options, but you have to specifically request them. They’re not always the default.

The track systems for motorized shades are another area where salt air matters. Aluminum tracks hold up well. Steel components in the motor housing should be sealed. I’ve had exactly one motor failure in a Davis Islands home that I traced back to salt corrosion in the motor housing, and it was a basic motor that didn’t have proper sealing. Since then, I’ve exclusively used Somfy motors for Davis Islands motorized installations. Their build quality and sealing are designed for coastal applications.

Hurricane Season Considerations

Davis Islands flooded during Hurricane Irma in 2017 and saw significant storm surge. Window treatments can’t prevent flooding, but they can help with the less dramatic weather events that happen every summer during hurricane season.

During tropical storms and weaker hurricanes, the big concerns for window treatments are wind-driven rain seeping around window frames and rapid pressure changes that can push blinds and shades off their mounts.

Inside-mounted window treatments handle pressure changes better than outside-mounted ones because the window frame itself provides structural support. For homes without impact glass, I recommend inside-mount composite shutters as the most weather-resistant option. Closed shutters provide an additional barrier against wind-driven debris, though they’re not rated as storm protection.

For homes with impact glass (most newer Davis Islands construction), the glass handles the storm protection, and you can choose window treatments based purely on aesthetics and daily function.

What I’d Do If I Lived on Davis Islands

If I bought a home on Davis Islands tomorrow, here’s exactly what I’d install:

West-facing windows: motorized solar roller shades, 3% openness, Insolroll fabric, PowerView system. Non-negotiable.

Main living areas: Norman composite plantation shutters for the architectural character and light control.

Bedrooms: Hunter Douglas Duette Architella with LightLock for complete darkness when needed, motorized for convenience.

Kitchen and bathrooms: Graber composite shutters or solar roller shades, depending on the window size and style.

Every motor in the house would be Somfy. Every piece of hardware would be corrosion-resistant. And I’d set up scenes and schedules on day one so the whole system runs itself.
