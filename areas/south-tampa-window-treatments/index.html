Window Treatments in South Tampa: What Your Neighbors Are Installing

Discover which window treatments South Tampa homeowners are choosing for historic bungalows, Mediterranean revivals, and new builds near Bayshore.

A couple on Bayshore Boulevard called me last spring about their 1940s Mediterranean revival home. They’d just closed on it, paid over a million, and the previous owners had left cheap aluminum mini blinds in every room. These gorgeous arched windows, original plaster archways, hand-laid tile floors. And dangling from those beautiful openings? Dollar-store blinds that looked like they’d survived three hurricanes and lost.

That pretty much sums up the South Tampa window treatment situation. You’ve got some of the most beautiful homes in the entire Tampa Bay area, and half of them still have builder-grade blinds or outdated verticals from the Clinton administration.

What Makes South Tampa Homes Different

South Tampa isn’t one thing. That’s what makes working in this area so interesting, and so challenging from a window treatment perspective.

Drive down Bayshore and you’ll see Mediterranean revivals from the 1920s and 1930s with thick stucco walls, deep-set windows, and those signature arched openings. Head over to the Palma Ceia neighborhood and you’re looking at mid-century ranches mixed with estate homes. The SoHo district has converted bungalows next to brand-new townhomes. Then there’s the new construction sprinkled throughout, with open floor plans and massive window walls.

Each of these home styles demands a completely different approach to window treatments.

The older homes, especially the ones from the 1920s through the 1960s, have window openings that aren’t standard sizes. Nothing is square. Nothing is plumb. I’ve measured windows in South Tampa bungalows where the top of the frame was a full half-inch wider than the bottom. Try fitting an off-the-shelf blind in that opening. It won’t happen.

New construction in South Tampa tends toward large sliding glass doors, floor-to-ceiling windows, and open great rooms where the afternoon sun turns your living room into a convection oven. Different problem, different solution.

The South Tampa Challenges Nobody Warns You About

Living near the water in South Tampa means dealing with things that homeowners in, say, Wesley Chapel never think about.

Humidity and salt air. Even if you’re not directly on Bayshore, the moisture levels in South Tampa are brutal on certain materials. Real wood blinds? They’ll warp within two years. I’ve pulled warped wooden blinds out of homes on Azeele Street and MacDill Avenue that looked like they’d been left in a sauna. Composite materials and faux wood are the smart play here.

Hurricane exposure. South Tampa sits on a peninsula. When storms roll through, you’re getting wind from multiple directions. Window treatments don’t replace hurricane shutters or impact glass, but the right products can handle the pressure changes and moisture that come with tropical weather. Cheap blinds rattle, break, and fall apart. Quality products with secure mounting stay put.

Afternoon sun intensity. West-facing windows in South Tampa are a whole different animal. The sun comes screaming off the bay in the afternoon, and if you’ve got hardwood floors or leather furniture near those windows, you’re watching your finishes fade in real time. UV-blocking cellular shades or solar roller shades aren’t optional here. They’re protection for everything in your home.

Irregular and arched windows. This is the big one. Probably 40% of the homes I work in across South Tampa have at least one non-standard window. Arched tops, eyebrow windows, half-rounds above the main windows. These require custom solutions. There’s no off-the-shelf fix.

Plantation shutters dominate in South Tampa, and honestly, they should. A well-made shutter in a Mediterranean revival home looks like it was always supposed to be there. The material matches the architectural weight of those thick walls and deep window sills. Norman and Hunter Douglas both make excellent options, but for South Tampa specifically, I lean toward Norman’s composite shutters. They handle the humidity without warping, and the finish quality is hard to beat.

For the older bungalows, especially the ones in the SoHo area, I’ve been installing a lot of Graber cellular shades. These homes often still have original single-pane windows, and the cellular construction creates an air pocket that genuinely helps with insulation. My clients notice the difference on their electric bills, especially during the summer when their AC is running around the clock.

Roman shades are having a moment in South Tampa right now. A lot of the interior designers working with remodelers in this area are specifying them for kitchens and bathrooms where shutters feel too heavy but something more polished than a roller shade is needed. The fabric options from Hunter Douglas and Graber give you everything from casual linen to structured formality.

The Bayshore Mediterranean Story

Let me come back to that couple I mentioned at the top. Mark and Elena had purchased their Mediterranean revival on Bayshore and were doing a full interior renovation. The architect they hired was great with the structural stuff but didn’t have strong opinions on window treatments. So they called me after finding our website.

The house had 22 windows total. Fourteen of them were standard rectangular openings, but eight had arched tops. Two of those arches were in the front-facing living room, visible from Bayshore Boulevard, and they were each about seven feet tall with a 28-inch radius arch at the top.

Here’s what a lot of companies would do: put a standard rectangular shutter or blind on the bottom portion and leave the arch uncovered, or stick a fixed sunburst shutter in the arch. Both options are fine, but for a home of this caliber, they’re lazy.

We went with full-height Norman plantation shutters with custom arched tops that followed the exact curve of each window. The shutters in the arched portion have a fixed louver set at a permanent 45-degree angle, while the rectangular sections below operate normally. The effect is stunning. The shutters look like they were built with the house in 1942.

For the bedrooms, Elena wanted something softer. We did Hunter Douglas Duette cellular shades with the LightLock system, which creates a near-total blackout. In a house where the morning sun bounces off the bay and floods the east-facing master bedroom, that blackout capability changed their sleep quality overnight. Literally.

The kitchen and breakfast nook got Graber solar roller shades in a light bronze tone that pulls the warm tones from the original tile floor. Functional, clean, and they don’t compete with all the architectural detail in the room.

Total project took about six weeks from measurement to installation. Mark told me later that the shutters were the single best decision they made in the entire renovation, ahead of the new kitchen counters and the refinished floors. That’s the kind of impact the right window treatments have in a home like this.

Why Professional Installation Matters in South Tampa

I get asked all the time whether DIY installation is an option. In some neighborhoods with newer, standard-construction homes, you could probably get away with it on basic products. South Tampa is not that neighborhood.

The older homes here have plaster walls, not drywall. You can’t just drill into plaster with a standard bit and expect it to hold. The anchoring technique is different, and if you crack the plaster around a window frame in a 1930s Mediterranean home, you’re looking at an expensive repair.

Then there are the window frames themselves. Original wood frames in South Tampa homes have often been painted over multiple times. The dimensions you measure on the inside of the frame might not reflect the actual mounting surface once you account for layers of paint. I’ve seen a quarter-inch of paint buildup on window sills in some of these older homes. That matters when you’re doing an inside-mount shutter that needs precise clearance.

The arched and specialty windows are a whole other conversation. Getting the template right for a custom arch requires experience. You need to create a precise pattern of the curve, because no two arches in a 1940s home are identical. The arch on the left side of the living room might have a slightly different radius than the one on the right. A professional installer measures each one individually and creates separate templates.

And for the new construction homes in South Tampa with those floor-to-ceiling windows and 20-foot ceilings in the living area? You need proper equipment. A six-foot stepladder from Home Depot isn’t going to cut it for mounting motorized roller shades at the top of a two-story window wall. We bring scaffolding and the right tools for the job.

The Motorization Wave in South Tampa

I’m seeing a significant shift toward motorized window treatments in South Tampa, and it makes total sense. These homes often have high or hard-to-reach windows where manually adjusting blinds is impractical. Nobody wants to drag out a step stool every morning to tilt the shutters in their stairway window.

Hunter Douglas PowerView is the system I recommend most. It integrates with most smart home platforms, and you can set schedules so the shades adjust automatically throughout the day. Morning sun hitting the breakfast nook at 7 AM? The shades lower to 50% before you even get out of bed. Afternoon glare off the bay pounding the west-facing living room? The solar shades drop at 3 PM on their own.

The convenience factor is real, but the comfort benefits are what actually justify the upgrade. When your window treatments respond to the sun’s position automatically, your AC doesn’t have to work as hard. In a South Tampa home where you’re running the AC eight or nine months out of the year, that adds up.

Product Recommendations by Home Type

If you’re in South Tampa and trying to narrow down your options, here’s my honest advice based on the home you’re in:

1920s-1960s Mediterranean/Spanish Revival: Norman composite plantation shutters for the main living areas, with custom arched tops for specialty windows. Cellular shades for bedrooms if you want blackout capability. Roman shades for kitchens and baths.

Mid-Century Ranch: Roller shades or cellular shades throughout for a clean, modern look that doesn’t fight the architecture. Plantation shutters on front-facing windows for curb appeal if you want that classic look.

SoHo Bungalows and Townhomes: Depends on the vibe. The renovated bungalows do well with a mix of Roman shades and shutters. New townhomes suit roller shades or layered shades from Hunter Douglas.

New Construction: Motorized roller shades or cellular shades for the large windows. Plantation shutters on smaller, more traditional windows. Consider whole-home motorization from the start. It’s easier and cheaper to wire everything during construction than to retrofit later.
