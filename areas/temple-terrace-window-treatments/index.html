Window Treatments in Temple Terrace: Classic Neighborhood, Fresh Look

Window treatment options for Temple Terrace homes. Faux wood blinds, cellular shades, and composite shutters for mid-century and updated homes near the Hillsborough River.

Window Treatments in Temple Terrace: Classic Neighborhood, Fresh Look

A woman in Temple Terrace called me a few months ago because her dining room looked “stuck in 1993.” Her words, not mine. She’d updated the kitchen counters, painted the walls, even refinished the original terrazzo floors that run through half the house. But the windows still had the same dusty rose mini blinds that were hanging when her parents bought the place in the early nineties. The funny thing was, she didn’t even notice them anymore. They’d become invisible to her. It took her sister visiting from Atlanta and saying “those blinds have to go” before she picked up the phone.

That story plays out constantly in Temple Terrace. This is a neighborhood where the bones are solid, the lots are generous, and the location is quietly excellent. But a lot of these homes are carrying window treatments that are two or three decades past their expiration date. If that’s your home, you’re not alone, and the fix is easier and more quality than you probably think.

The Temple Terrace Home Profile

Temple Terrace has a personality that’s distinct from the rest of Tampa. It was originally developed in the 1920s as a golf course community, and that country club heritage still shows in the tree-lined streets and the generous setbacks. The homes are a mix of original mid-century builds from the 1950s and 1960s, Florida ranch-style homes from the 1970s and 1980s, and some newer construction scattered throughout.

The mid-century homes are the ones that keep me busy. They tend to have aluminum-frame windows (sometimes the originals, sometimes replaced once in the ’90s), standard-sized openings, and rooms that aren’t huge by modern standards. Natural light matters in these homes because the rooms aren’t always large enough to absorb a dark or heavy window treatment without feeling closed in.

There’s also a strong community around Temple Terrace Golf and Country Club, with homes that range from modest to quite upscale. And the neighborhoods closer to the Hillsborough River, particularly along Riverhills Drive and the surrounding streets, have some genuinely beautiful properties with large windows overlooking mature landscaping.

The common thread across most Temple Terrace homes is this: they were built during an era when windows were practical and uniform. Standard sizes, standard placements. Which is actually great news for window treatments, because it means most products fit well without extensive customization, and whole-house projects stay within a reasonable their plan.

Faux Wood Blinds: The Smart Starting Point

If your Temple Terrace home still has metal mini blinds, cheap vinyl verticals, or some other relic from a past decade, faux wood blinds are the fastest and most quality upgrade that makes a real visual difference.

I know blinds don’t sound exciting. They’re not going to end up on a Pinterest board. But quality faux wood blinds, not the cheap ones from the hardware store, are a legitimately good product. Graber’s faux wood line has a realistic woodgrain texture, comes in a range of stain colors and whites, and the 2-inch slat gives you a much more modern look than those skinny 1-inch mini blinds you’re probably staring at right now.

For a standard Temple Terrace ranch-style home with 12-15 windows, faux wood blinds are competitively priced when installed. That puts a whole-house project in the range. You could do the entire house for less than what some people spend on shutters for one room.

And here’s what matters most in Florida: faux wood blinds handle humidity without warping, discoloring, or peeling. The composite material doesn’t absorb moisture the way real wood does, which means your bathroom blinds will look the same in year five as they did on installation day. I can’t say that about real wood products in a Florida home unless the climate control is perfect.

I did a whole-house faux wood project on a 1970s ranch near Temple Terrace Elementary last year. Fifteen windows, all 2-inch faux wood in a warm white. The total was just under installed, and the homeowner’s reaction when we finished the last window was worth the whole trip. She walked through the house room by room and kept saying, “It looks so clean.” That’s exactly what faux wood blinds do. They make a house look pulled together and cared for.

Cellular Shades: comfort benefits That Actually Matter

Temple Terrace sits a little further inland than the coastal communities, but it still gets the full force of Florida’s summer heat. Those mid-century homes with single-pane aluminum windows? They’re basically greenhouses from May through October. Your AC is working overtime to compensate for the heat pouring through every window.

Cellular shades, sometimes called honeycomb shades, are the most energy-efficient window treatment on the market. The honeycomb structure traps air in pockets that insulate against heat transfer. Single-cell shades provide moderate insulation. Double-cell shades provide significantly more. For a Temple Terrace home with older windows, cellular shades can reduce heat gain through windows by 40-60%, depending on the product and the window orientation.

Hunter Douglas Duette is the gold standard in cellular shades. They pioneered the honeycomb design and their product quality is still the benchmark everyone else chases. The light-filtering option lets diffused light through while blocking UV and heat. The blackout option blocks everything, which is fantastic for bedrooms.

For a Temple Terrace homeowner who’s energy-conscious, I usually recommend cellular shades throughout the home with blackout in bedrooms and light-filtering everywhere else. The whole-house price for a 15-window home on the brand and options. That’s more than faux wood blinds, but the comfort benefits are real. I’ve had customers tell me their electric bill noticeably each month after installation. Over a few years, the shades partially pay for themselves.

A retired teacher on Glen Arven Avenue did exactly this project last summer. Her 1962 home had the original jalousie windows in the Florida room (which she kept because she loves the airflow) and replacement vinyl windows elsewhere. We put Hunter Douglas Duette double-cell shades on every window except the Florida room, where we did a simple roller shade that could handle the occasional rain blowing in. Her July electric bill was less than the previous July. She sends me a photo of her bill every month with a smiley face. I’m not making that up.

Composite Shutters for the Showpiece Rooms

Not every room in a Temple Terrace home needs shutters. But the rooms that guests see first? Shutters transform them.

The living room, dining room, and kitchen in a Temple Terrace home are where composite shutters make the biggest impact. They add architectural substance to windows that might otherwise look plain, and they project a level of quality that visitors notice immediately. In a mid-century home where the windows are standard rectangles with modest trim, shutters actually add the architectural detail that the original builders left out.

Norman’s Woodlore Plus composite shutter is my default recommendation for Temple Terrace homes. The quality is consistent, and the product handles Florida conditions without complaint. For homes near the golf course or along the river, Hunter Douglas NewStyle or real basswood shutters from Norman’s premium line are worth considering. They add genuine resale value, and discerning homeowners notice the difference between composite and wood.

The smart play for most Temple Terrace homeowners is the hybrid approach. Composite shutters on the front-facing windows and the main living areas, cellular shades or faux wood blinds in the bedrooms and secondary spaces. This gets you the visual impact where it counts and keeps the total project manageable. For a typical three-bedroom home, a hybrid project runs which is about half of what whole-house shutters would cost.

Those Original Windows Need Careful Measurement

One thing I’ve learned working in Temple Terrace is to never assume anything about the windows in a mid-century home. Some have been replaced once or twice over the decades, some are still original, and some are a mix of both because a previous owner replaced the front windows for curb appeal but left the back of the house alone.

Original aluminum-frame windows from the 1950s and 1960s have slightly different depth dimensions than modern vinyl replacement windows. The frame depth affects whether an inside-mount treatment will fit properly. I’ve shown up to install blinds in Temple Terrace homes where the previous company measured without checking the frame depth, and the product didn’t fit. That’s a waste of everyone’s time and money.

Proper measurement means checking every window individually, verifying the frame depth at multiple points (because old frames can be slightly bowed or out of square), and confirming whether the window operates correctly so the treatment doesn’t interfere with opening and closing. It takes longer than measuring a brand-new home where every window is identical. But it’s the difference between a finished product that looks right and one that looks like an afterthought.

Temple Terrace Doesn’t Need to Look Like Every Other Neighborhood

I think what I like most about working in Temple Terrace is that the homes have personality. They’re not cookie-cutter. A 1958 ranch with terrazzo floors and jalousie windows has a different energy than a 2005 new build, and the window treatments should reflect that. You don’t need to follow whatever trend is dominating Instagram right now. You need products that fit your windows correctly, suit the style of your home, and solve the practical problems of living in a Florida house that might be six decades old.

Good window treatments can bridge the gap between “outdated” and “classic.” Temple Terrace homes have the classic part built in. The right treatments just bring it forward.
