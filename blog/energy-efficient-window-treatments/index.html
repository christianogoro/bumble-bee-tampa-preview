7 Energy-Efficient Window Treatments That Actually Lower Your Electric Bill

Window treatments can cut comfort levels by 25-30%. Here are the 7 best options for blocking heat, reducing AC costs, and saving money.

7 Energy-Efficient Window Treatments That Actually Lower Your Electric Bill

I had a customer in South Tampa last August. Nice family, 2,400 square foot house, electric bill. Three hundred and eighty dollars in one month. She called because she’d read somewhere that window treatments could help with energy wanted to know if it was real or just marketing.

It’s real.

The U.S. Department of Energy says 25 to 30% of residential heating and cooling energy goes straight through your windows. Heat gain, heat loss, the glass doesn’t care. In Florida, where AC accounts for roughly 40% of your electric bill, that’s serious money bleeding through every pane of glass every single day.

The right window treatments can reduce heat gain by up to 77%. That’s DOE data, not something I made up. Here are the seven treatments that actually make a difference, starting with the best.

1. Cellular (Honeycomb) Shades: The Clear Winner for Interior Treatments

If I could only install one product in every Florida home, this would be it. (Fair warning: exterior shading, which I cover at #5, technically outperforms everything on this list. But most people want interior solutions, and that, cellular shades are unmatched.)

You’ve probably seen these. They have that accordion-fold honeycomb shape. That shape isn’t just aesthetic.

Each “cell” traps a pocket of dead air, and dead air is one of the best insulators that exists. Same principle behind double-pane windows, insulated walls, even a Yeti cooler.

Single-cell shades have one layer of air pockets. Double-cell shades stack two layers. The difference matters.

The numbers:

  • Single-cell: R-value 2 to 3
  • Double-cell: R-value 4 to 5
  • A standard double-pane window? R-value of about 2.

So a double-cell honeycomb shade effectively more than doubles your window’s insulation.

Are double-cell worth the extra money? In Florida, absolutely yes. The DOE estimates cellular shades cut heat gain by up to 60%, and double-cell versions push that toward 80% on south-facing windows. The upgrade costs 15-25% more. You’ll make it back in comfort benefits within a couple of cooling seasons.

What this actually looks like on a bill: That South Tampa customer I mentioned? We put double-cell cellulars on her 15 worst windows, all south and west facing. Her August bill the following year was . Almost a hundred dollars less per month at the peak of summer.

Over one cooling season (April through October in Tampa, basically), she saved around . The shades paid for themselves in under two years.

Best brands: Hunter Douglas Duette Architella is the gold standard for double-cell. Pricey but worth it. Graber CrystalPleat is excellent at a more accessible tier. Bali DiamondCell is solid. If you’re on a tight your home, Select Blinds honeycomb shades get the job done.

Cost: Varies

2. Solar Shades: Built for Exactly This Problem

Cellular shades trap air to insulate. Solar shades take a completely different approach: they filter sunlight before it becomes a problem. They’re made from a mesh fabric specifically engineered to reject solar energy while still letting you see outside. Stand at a window with solar shades down and you can see your yard, your pool, the neighbor’s questionable landscaping choices. Stand outside looking in and you can’t see anything.

The openness factor is the key spec. It’s measured from 1% to 14%, and lower means more blocking. For energy efficiency, stay between 1% and 5%. I’ve seen people get 10% or 14% because they want more visibility, and then they’re disappointed that their room is still hot. Lower is better for heat. Period.

The performance (and an important distinction):

  • Block up to 95% of UV rays
  • Reduce solar heat gain by 40 to 65% depending on fabric color and openness factor
  • Cut glare by 75 to 95%

A quick note here. You’ll see some manufacturers claim solar shades “block 65 to 90% of solar heat.” That number is actually their UV rejection rate, not total solar heat gain reduction. UV is a big part of the equation, but it’s not the whole picture. The real heat reduction depends on fabric density, color (darker absorbs more), and that openness factor. Still very effective, just be precise when you’re comparing specs.

Why Florida people love these: They block the heat without turning your room into a dungeon. Blackout shades work great for energy, but then you’re sitting in the dark at 2pm. Solar shades keep the natural light, keep the view, and still knock the heat down dramatically.

I install a ton of these in Bayshore condos and Davis Islands homes with water views. Nobody wants to block a million-dollar view with opaque shades.

Best rooms: sunrooms, Florida rooms (every Florida home has one, and they’re all sweltering), west-facing living rooms, and home offices where glare on your screen drives you crazy.

Best brands: Phifer SheerWeave fabric is the industry standard. If the shade uses SheerWeave, it’s good. Hunter Douglas Designer Screen and Graber Solar Shades are also solid.

3. Plantation Shutters: The Surprise Energy Player

Nobody walks into my showroom and says “I want the most energy-efficient shutter you’ve got.” Shutters sell because they look incredible and boost home value. The energy performance is a bonus most people don’t even know about until I bring it up.

But the physics are solid. When you close the louvers fully, the shutter panel plus the air gap between it and the glass creates an insulation pocket. R-value of 3 to 4 when closed. Heat transfer drops 45 to 50%. That air gap alone adds an effective R-value of 1 to 2.

Here’s what really sets shutters apart from everything else on this list, though. They’re adjustable in real time, all day long. Angle the louvers to bounce sunlight toward the ceiling and you get free ambient light without direct heat blasting into the room. When the afternoon sun hits its worst (3 to 5pm on west-facing windows here in Tampa), close them completely. When the sun shifts, open them back up.

You’re manually optimizing comfort benefits throughout the day. No other treatment gives you that level of granular control without going motorized.

And unlike shades that depreciate the moment you install them, shutters are the only window treatment that adds to your resale price. A Tampa window treatment pro like Bumble Bee Blinds can walk you through composite vs. basswood options and which makes the most sense for your home’s sun exposure.

Cost: Varies

4. Insulated Roman Shades: The Good-Looking Overachiever

Shutters deliver comfort benefits as a bonus on top of their looks. Roman shades take a similar path, but with one critical caveat: not all of them actually do anything for energy.

Roman shades are everywhere right now. Designers love them, Pinterest loves them, and they’re one of the most popular treatments I install. A standard Roman shade, though, is just fabric folded into pleats. Pretty, but thermally useless with an R-value of 1 to 2.

The insulated version is where things get interesting. Behind the decorative fabric sits a thermal lining, either reflective or foam-backed material, that reflects heat back toward the window in summer. That single addition transforms the shade from decorative to functional.

The performance gap is dramatic:

  • Standard (unlined): R-value 1 to 2
  • Thermally lined: R-value 3 to 4
  • Blackout-lined with thermal backing: R-value 4 to 5

Here’s the mistake I see constantly. When you’re ordering, you have to specifically ask for “thermal lining” or “insulated lining.” If you just order a Roman shade, you’ll get a pretty piece of fabric and zero energy benefit. I’ve had customers come to me frustrated that their Roman shades “don’t work,” and it turns out they got unlined ones. The lining is what does the heavy lifting.

Best for: Living rooms and bedrooms where you want that soft, textured look but also want the shade to pull its weight on your comfort levels.

Cost: Varies

5. Exterior Shades and Awnings: The Move Most People Don’t Think About

I ranked cellular shades #1 because they’re the best interior solution, and most homeowners are shopping for something inside their house. But I need to be straight with you: nothing inside your home can match what exterior shading does.

Here’s the physics. Once sunlight passes through glass, it’s already inside and already heating your room. Interior treatments can only manage heat that’s already entered. Exterior shading intercepts it before it ever touches the glass. That’s a fundamentally different approach, and the performance gap shows.

How much better? Exterior shading blocks up to 90% of solar heat at the source, compared to 60-77% for the best interior treatments. If your only goal is maximum comfort benefits and you don’t care about aesthetics, exterior shading wins outright.

A house in Westchase I worked on last year had a screened-in Florida room on the west side that was unusable after 2pm from April through October. Interior shades helped but didn’t solve it. We added exterior solar screens That room went from “storage” to “where we eat dinner every night.”

So why isn’t this #1? Two reasons. First, HOA restrictions. Many Tampa-area communities won’t allow visible exterior modifications, and that eliminates awnings and external rollers for a lot of homeowners. Second, exterior treatments don’t give you the interior light control, privacy, or design options that shades and shutters provide. Most people end up combining exterior screening with interior treatments for the best of both worlds.

Your options:

Retractable awnings ( to ) cover windows and patios, available in manual or motorized. Motorized versions with sun sensors can deploy automatically when UV hits a threshold. Sunsetter and SunSpa are the brands I see most in the Tampa Bay area.

Exterior roller shades ( These are popular in commercial buildings and are starting to show up in residential. They look clean and modern when installed right.

Solar screens ( Cheapest option by far, and incredibly effective for the price. You can DIY these in an afternoon with a kit from Home Depot.

Best for: West-facing walls that get destroyed by afternoon sun, Florida rooms, patio areas, and anywhere interior treatments alone aren’t getting the job done.

6. Reflective Blinds: The Value Play

Not everyone has a large amount I get that. Reflective blinds offer a real step up from standard blinds at basically the same tier.

They work by bouncing solar radiation back through the glass before it heats your room. One side of each slat has a metallic or reflective coating that acts like a heat mirror. You face that reflective side outward, and a meaningful chunk of solar energy gets redirected before it becomes your AC’s problem.

Performance:

  • Reduce heat gain by 35 to 50%
  • Most effective on south and west-facing windows during peak hours
  • Work best when kept closed or nearly closed

These aren’t going to compete with cellular shades or solar shades on raw performance. But For a homeowner in Wesley Chapel or Riverview with 20 windows and limited resources, reflective blinds on the worst sun-facing windows can trim your electric bill to per month without a major commitment.

I’ve used them as a stopgap for clients who plan to do cellular shades eventually but need something better than builder-grade right now. They’re also a solid choice for rental properties where you want energy performance without the premium price tag.

Best brands: Levolor and Bali both offer reflective aluminum blinds at reasonable tier. For a more polished look, 2-inch faux wood blinds with reflective backing split the difference between energy function and aesthetics.

7. Layered Treatments: The “I’m Done Messing Around” Option

Everything I’ve listed so far is a single product doing one job. Layering puts two treatments on the same window and lets them tag-team the heat problem. The results get close to exterior shading performance without touching the outside of your house.

The concept is straightforward. Pair a close-to-glass treatment (cellular shade, roller shade, or solar shade) with a room-side treatment (drapes, curtains, or shutters). The inner layer blocks heat at the glass. The outer layer creates a second air barrier and adds another line of defense. Together they outperform either one alone by a significant margin.

Combinations that work:

  • Cellular shade + lined drapes: R-value 6 to 8. Excellent.
  • Solar shade + sheer curtains: R-value 3 to 4 plus UV blocking
  • Roller shade inside + plantation shutters outside: R-value 5 to 7

These combos reduce heat transfer by 70 to 85%. For context, that’s competitive with the 90% you’d get from exterior shading, and it looks a whole lot better from inside.

I installed a layered setup in a South Tampa home last fall. The living room had a massive west-facing picture window that was cooking the house every afternoon. We put double-cell cellular shades tight against the glass and hung heavy linen drapes on a rod about four inches in front. The homeowner told me the room felt like a different house. Her AC stopped cycling constantly around 3pm for the first time since she’d moved in.

Designers love this approach too. Layering isn’t just functional, it adds visual depth and texture to a room. A simple roller shade paired with floor-length drapes reads as intentional and polished in a way that a single treatment rarely does.

Best for: Living rooms with big windows, bedrooms where you want maximum darkness and insulation, and any room where you’ve tried one treatment and it’s not enough.

What I Tell Every Florida Homeowner

Knowing the products is one thing. Knowing where to start is what actually saves you money. Here’s the advice I give every client after we walk their home.

Hit your west-facing windows first. A free energy assessment from Bumble Bee Blinds can identify exactly which windows are costing you the most, but as a rule of thumb: south and west windows catch the most direct sunlight, and the west side is worse because it gets hammered during the hottest part of the day. In Tampa, that west wall takes brutal afternoon sun from March through October, basically eight months of the year. Treating just the west-facing windows can get you 40-50% of the total comfort benefits you’d get from doing the whole house. Best bang for your buck by far.

Don’t ignore sliding glass doors. They’re some of the biggest glass surfaces in Florida homes and everyone forgets about them. Vertical cellular shades, panel track blinds with solar fabric, or even a well-placed exterior shade sail can make a massive difference.

Automate if you can. Motorized shades programmed to close from 10am to 4pm on sun-facing windows deliver consistent savings without you having to remember anything. Set it and forget it. The upfront cost pays for itself through comfort benefits you’d never capture manually, because let’s be real, nobody remembers to close their blinds at 10am every morning.

Combo move: window film + cellular shades. 3M Prestige or similar UV-blocking film applied directly to the glass, plus cellular shades on top. The film blocks UV and some heat passively even when the shades are open, and the shades add insulation when closed. This is the ultimate Florida window setup.

Check for utility rebates. Some Florida utility providers offer rebates for energy-efficient home improvements including window treatments. Five-minute phone call, potentially hundreds of dollars back. I’ve had customers get back from Duke Energy. Worth asking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most energy-efficient window treatment?

Double-cell cellular (honeycomb) shades are the most energy-efficient interior window treatment, with an R-value of 4-5 and the ability to reduce heat gain by up to 80%. For comparison, a standard double-pane window has an R-value of about 2, so these shades more than double your window’s insulation. For Tampa Bay homes, installing double-cell cellulars on south and west-facing windows can reduce monthly heat gain by during peak summer months.

What window treatments work best in Florida’s humidity?

Composite plantation shutters, faux wood blinds, and vinyl treatments work best in Florida’s humidity because they are engineered to resist moisture absorption, warping, and mildew. Avoid real wood in bathrooms and kitchens where humidity is highest. For dry, air-conditioned rooms, real wood treatments are fine because your AC controls the ambient moisture. In the Tampa Bay area, faux wood is the most popular choice for its humidity resistance and natural wood appearance.

The best window treatments for Tampa Bay homes are faux wood blinds (value), cellular shades (energy efficiency), and composite shutters (durability and resale value), chosen for their resistance to Florida’s humidity, UV exposure, and heat. How long do window treatments last in Florida?

Window treatments typically last 5-25 years in Florida when properly installed and maintained. Florida’s intense UV and humidity can shorten the lifespan of lower-quality materials by 1-3 years compared to northern climates. Choosing humidity-resistant materials like composite, faux wood, or vinyl and investing in quality brands significantly extends product life in the Tampa Bay climate.
