Hurricane Season Window Prep: What Tampa Homeowners Need to Do

June 1 to November 30 is hurricane season. Here's what Tampa homeowners need to know about protecting their windows, window treatments, and homes during storms.

Hurricane Season Window Prep: What Tampa Homeowners Need to Do

A family on Davis Islands rode out Hurricane Ian in September 2022. They’d lived in Tampa for six years and had never taken a direct hit, so they figured their beautiful plantation shutters would provide some protection. The shutters were real wood, custom-stained to match their cabinetry. They The storm surge didn’t reach their house, thankfully. But a piece of debris hit their front window hard enough to crack the outer pane. Wind-driven rain got in through two windows where the seals had been marginal for years. The shutters? The interior shutters did nothing to stop any of it. They weren’t designed to. They’re window treatments, not storm protection.

After the storm, two of their shutter panels had water damage from the rain intrusion. The stain was ruined. One panel warped. Repair cost: .

This story plays out across Tampa every hurricane season, and it comes from a fundamental misunderstanding. Interior window treatments are not storm protection. They never have been. And confusing the two can cost you thousands in damage.

The Basics: June 1 Through November 30

Hurricane season runs six months. In Tampa Bay, we tend to get complacent because we went from 1921 to 2024 without a major direct hit. Over a hundred years. That stretch of luck made a lot of people treat hurricane prep as optional. Helene and Milton in 2024 changed that conversation permanently.

Here’s what NOAA says about the Tampa Bay area specifically: we’re one of the most vulnerable metro areas in the country for storm surge. The shape of Tampa Bay acts like a funnel, amplifying surge. A Category 3 storm hitting at the right angle could push 10-15 feet of water into low-lying areas of South Tampa, Davis Islands, Shore Acres, and Bayshore.

None of that has anything to do with window treatments directly. But it sets the context for why you need to take window prep seriously. When a storm comes, your windows are the most vulnerable part of your home’s envelope. If a window fails, your home pressurizes, and that’s when roofs come off.

Interior Shutters Are NOT Hurricane Protection

I need to be very clear about this because I get asked at least once a month: plantation shutters, interior wood shutters, composite shutters, any interior shutter product is decorative. They control light, provide privacy, and look beautiful. They do not protect your windows from flying debris.

The force of debris in a hurricane is extraordinary. A 2x4 traveling at 50 mph (which happens in a Category 2 storm) will go through a standard window like it’s not there. Your interior shutters are on the wrong side of the glass. By the time debris reaches them, the window is already broken and your home’s envelope is compromised.

I bring this up not to scare anyone, but because I’ve had homeowners tell me they felt “protected” because they had shutters. That false confidence can be dangerous if it means you skip proper storm preparation.

What Actually Protects Your Windows

Real hurricane protection goes on the outside of your home, and it needs to meet Miami-Dade or Florida Building Code impact ratings. Here are your actual options:

Accordion Hurricane Shutters

These are permanently mounted on tracks beside your windows. When a storm approaches, you pull them across and lock them. They’re aluminum, impact-rated, and they work. Not pretty, but effective and quality. Most Tampa homes with hurricane shutters use accordion style.

These are the premium option. They roll up into a housing above the window and deploy with a crank or motor. When they’re up, they’re nearly invisible. When they’re down, they provide full impact protection. Motorized versions can be deployed from inside the house, which matters when you’re prepping with rain already starting.

Cost: Full-house installations often ### Hurricane Panels

The most quality option. These are corrugated aluminum or clear polycarbonate panels that you bolt into pre-installed tracks around each window. They work great but they require manual installation for each storm, which means you’re outside with a drill and a ladder while the sky turns green. Not ideal.

Impact-Rated Windows

This is the gold standard. Impact windows have a laminated inner layer (like a car windshield) that holds together even when the outer glass breaks. No shutters needed, no prep before a storm, full-time protection. They also reduce noise, improve energy efficiency, and can lower your insurance premiums significantly.

But you never have to prep for a storm, and your insurance savings can be substantial.

What Hillsborough County Code Requires

If you’re doing any window work in Hillsborough County (and that covers most of Tampa proper), the Florida Building Code requires that new construction and major renovations include wind-borne debris protection in the Wind-Borne Debris Region. Tampa falls within this zone.

That means if you’re replacing windows, building new, or doing a major remodel, you’ll need either impact-rated windows or an approved shutter system. This isn’t optional. It’s code.

For existing homes with no modifications, there’s no retroactive requirement. But your insurance company may offer discounts for adding approved hurricane protection. Check with your insurer before you decide which protection system to install. It may influence your decision.

How to Protect Your Window Treatments Before a Storm

Now let’s talk about the stuff I actually install for a living. Your interior window treatments need protection during a hurricane, not the other way around. Here’s a practical checklist.

Before Hurricane Season (May)

Inspect your windows. Look for cracked seals, gaps in weatherstripping, and any signs of moisture intrusion. Fix these before storm season starts. A window that leaks a little in a normal rain will fail badly in hurricane-force wind-driven rain.

Photograph everything. Take photos of every window treatment in your home. Close-ups of the hardware, the fabric, the finish. Store these photos in the cloud (not just on your phone). This is your insurance documentation. If you need to file a claim, having before photos makes the process dramatically smoother.

Check your hurricane protection system. If you have accordion shutters, make sure the tracks are clean and lubricated. If you have panels, verify you have all the wing nuts and bolts and that they’re not corroded. Do this in May, not when a storm is three days away and the hardware store is sold out of everything.

When a Storm Is Approaching (72-48 Hours Out)

Raise all window treatments to the fully open/up position. This seems counterintuitive, but it’s important. If wind-driven rain gets past your windows (which is common even without a full window failure), you want your shades, blinds, and shutters up and away from the water. A cellular shade that sits in water for hours will be ruined. Roller shades, same thing. Get them up.

Remove any freestanding or lightweight treatments. If you have Roman shades that simply unhook, take them down and store them in a closet or interior room. Same for curtains and drapes on simple rod systems. If removal takes 30 seconds If it’s a complex installation that would require tools and an hour

Close your interior shutters if you have them. Wait, didn’t I just say interior shutters aren’t storm protection? Correct. But closing them provides one benefit: if a window cracks but doesn’t fully break, closed shutters can prevent glass fragments from spraying into the room. They’re a secondary barrier, not a primary one. And if water comes in, composite or faux wood shutters handle moisture far better than real wood.

Move furniture away from windows. This protects both the furniture and the window treatments. If a window fails and wind enters the room, anything near the window becomes a projectile.

After the Storm

Don’t lower your window treatments immediately. Check every window first. Look for cracks, broken seals, water between panes, and moisture on the sills. If a window took damage but your treatments are safely raised above it, keep them up until the window is repaired or replaced.

Check for moisture damage. Even if your windows held, high humidity during and after the storm can affect wood treatments. Real wood shutters and blinds can absorb moisture from the air alone during a multi-day storm event. Check for warping, swelling, or stain discoloration.

Document any damage before cleaning up. If you find damage, photograph it thoroughly before you clean, repair, or remove anything. Your insurance adjuster needs to see the damage as it occurred.

Insurance Considerations for Window Treatments

Most homeowners don’t realize that window treatments are covered under their homeowner’s policy as part of the dwelling or personal property coverage. But there are some things you need to know.

Standard HO-3 policies typically cover window treatment damage caused by windstorm, including hurricane damage. But there’s usually a separate hurricane deductible (often 2-5% of the dwelling coverage), and that deductible applies to all hurricane damage combined, not per item.

Replacement value vs. actual cash value matters enormously for window treatments. If you have actual cash value coverage, your 8-year-old Hunter Douglas shutters will be depreciated to a fraction of their replacement cost. If you have replacement value coverage, you get what it buy new ones. Check your policy. If you have custom window treatments worth or more, make sure you have replacement value coverage.

Keep receipts. I tell every client this. Keep the receipt from your window treatment installation, keep the warranty documentation, and keep those pre-storm photos. A clear paper trail makes insurance claims straightforward.

Scheduled personal property endorsements can be added to your policy for high-value window treatments (like a whole-house shutter installation). This provides broader coverage and often eliminates the deductible for those specific items. Talk to your agent.

Which Window Treatments Survive Florida Storms Best?

Not all treatments are equally resilient when things go sideways. Here’s an honest ranking based on what I’ve seen after storms over the years.

Most resilient: Composite and faux wood shutters. They handle moisture, don’t warp, and can be wiped down even after exposure to water. If your window holds but lets some rain in, composite shutters will be fine.

Very resilient: Faux wood blinds, vinyl blinds, aluminum blinds. Moisture isn’t a problem for these materials. The risk is physical damage from debris or pressure, but the materials themselves survive water exposure.

Moderately resilient: Roller shades (depending on fabric). Vinyl and PVC roller shades handle moisture well. Fabric rollers can develop mold or mildew if they get wet and aren’t dried quickly.

Vulnerable: Real wood shutters, real wood blinds, woven wood/natural shades. Wood absorbs water. Period. If these get wet, you’re likely looking at warping, staining, and potential mold issues.

Most vulnerable: Cellular shades, Roman shades, drapes, and curtains. Fabric treatments that absorb water are the hardest to salvage. Cellular shades in particular trap moisture inside the honeycomb cells, making them nearly impossible to fully dry.

A Note About Outdoor Shades and Storms

If you have exterior roller shades, retractable awnings, or motorized screen systems on your lanai or patio, retract everything before a storm. These products are designed for sun, normal rain, and moderate wind. They are not designed for hurricane-force conditions.

Good motorized systems have wind sensors that auto-retract at 25-35 mph. But don’t rely on the sensor alone during a hurricane. Manually retract everything 48 hours before projected impact. If you have fixed solar screens on exterior windows, remove them if possible. They can become projectiles in extreme wind.

The Bottom Line

Hurricane prep isn’t glamorous. It’s not fun. And for most of the years you live in Tampa, you’ll do all this prep and the storm will miss or weaken or turn. You’ll feel like you wasted a Saturday afternoon.

But the one time it doesn’t miss, you’ll be glad you did every item on that checklist. Protect your windows with proper exterior hurricane protection. Protect your window treatments by getting them out of harm’s way. Document everything for insurance.

And if you’re thinking about new window treatments and you’re weighing real wood versus composite, let me nudge you toward composite. Not just for hurricanes, but for Florida’s year-round humidity. Composite shutters and faux wood blinds handle everything this climate throws at them. Real wood is gorgeous, but it’s high-maintenance in Florida, storm or no storm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What window treatments work best in Florida’s humidity?

Composite plantation shutters, faux wood blinds, and vinyl treatments work best in Florida’s humidity because they are engineered to resist moisture absorption, warping, and mildew. Avoid real wood in bathrooms and kitchens where humidity is highest. For dry, air-conditioned rooms, real wood treatments are fine because your AC controls the ambient moisture. In the Tampa Bay area, faux wood is the most popular choice for its humidity resistance and natural wood appearance.

What are the best plantation shutters for Tampa Bay homes?

The best plantation shutters for Tampa Bay homes are composite shutters (best for humidity), basswood shutters (premium look), and vinyl shutters (practical for bathrooms), chosen for their resistance to Florida’s humidity, UV exposure, and heat. Hunter Douglas, Norman, and Graber are the top brands, with composite options delivering the best value for Florida’s climate. For a personalized recommendation based on your specific windows and sun exposure, Bumble Bee Blinds offers free in-home consultations with real product samples.

How long do plantation shutters last in Florida?

Plantation shutters typically last 15-25 years in Florida when properly installed and maintained. Florida’s intense UV and humidity can shorten the lifespan of lower-quality materials by 1-3 years compared to northern climates. Choosing humidity-resistant materials like composite, faux wood, or vinyl and investing in quality brands significantly extends product life in the Tampa Bay climate.

How do I clean my window treatments?

Most window treatments can be cleaned with a microfiber cloth or light vacuuming with a brush attachment. For blinds and shutters, wipe each slat with a damp cloth. For fabric shades, use a handheld vacuum on low suction. Avoid soaking real wood treatments. In Tampa Bay, clean your treatments every 2-3 months to prevent dust and pollen buildup that accumulates faster in Florida’s climate. Plantation shutters are the easiest to maintain, requiring only a quick damp wipe.

When should I replace my window treatments?

Replace your window treatments when you notice visible warping, yellowing, broken slats, frayed cords, or mechanisms that no longer operate smoothly. Most blinds last 5-8 years, cellular shades 7-10 years, and plantation shutters 15-25+ years. In Florida, cheap vinyl and aluminum treatments may need replacement sooner due to UV degradation and humidity damage. If your treatments are builder-grade originals from when your home was built, they are almost certainly overdue for an upgrade.
